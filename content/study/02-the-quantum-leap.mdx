# 2. The Quantum Leap

Nature isn't classical. At the microscopic level—the level of atoms and molecules—the universe doesn't operate in definite 0s and 1s.

If we want to simulate a new medicine, or design a new material for better batteries, we have to simulate the atoms. But because atoms exist in complex, swirling clouds of probability, simulating even a few hundred atoms perfectly would require a classical computer larger than the known universe!

As physicist Richard Feynman said in 1981:
> *"Nature isn't classical, dammit, and if you want to make a simulation of nature, you'd better make it quantum mechanical."*

To simulate nature, we need to build a computer out of nature's own building blocks. We need a Quantum Computer.
