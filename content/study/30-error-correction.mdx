# 30. Error Correction

If Shor's algorithm was discovered in 1994, why aren't quantum computers cracking all our passwords today?

The answer is **Decoherence**. 
Superposition is incredibly fragile. The slightest bump from a stray air molecule, a tiny fluctuation in temperature, or even a random cosmic ray can act as a "measurement", destroying the quantum state and crashing the calculation.

Classically, we fix errors by keeping backup copies of our data. But the **No-Cloning Theorem** says we cannot copy a quantum state! 

To fix this, physicists developed Quantum Error Correction. Instead of trying to copy a single qubit, they spread its information across dozens or hundreds of "physical" qubits using entanglement to create one stable "logical" qubit. By carefully measuring the edges of this web without measuring the precious data inside, they can detect and fix errors before they destroy the calculation.

Building these massive, error-corrected quantum computers is the grand challenge of the 21st century!
