# 19. The No-Cloning Theorem

In classical programming, if you have a variable `A`, you can easily say `B = A`. You just copied the data. `Ctrl+C`, `Ctrl+V`.

In quantum computing, this is a physical impossibility. 
Because of the mathematics governing quantum mechanics, there is absolutely no way to perfectly copy an unknown quantum state. 

This is known as the **No-Cloning Theorem**. 

If you have a qubit in a beautiful, complex superposition, you cannot make a backup of it. If you try to read it to copy it, measurement destroys the superposition. If you try to copy it using quantum gates without reading it, the math simply doesn't allow it.

This makes quantum computers incredibly difficult to error-correct, but it also makes them the ultimate secure communication device. If someone tries to copy your quantum message, the laws of physics will stop them.
