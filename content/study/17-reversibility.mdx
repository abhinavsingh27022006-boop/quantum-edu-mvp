# 17. Reversibility

In a classical computer, we have the `AND` gate. It takes two inputs, and produces one output (e.g., 1 AND 1 = 1). 
Because it takes two inputs and crushes them into one, information is lost. If I hand you the output `0` from an AND gate, you can never know if the inputs were `0,0`, `0,1`, or `1,0`.

Quantum computing forbids this.

One of the fundamental laws of quantum physics is that **information can never be destroyed**. Therefore, every single quantum gate must be perfectly reversible. 

If you apply an H gate, applying another H gate undoes it.
If you apply a CNOT, applying another CNOT undoes it!

Because quantum computers cannot destroy information, they also generate far less heat than classical computers when running operations.
