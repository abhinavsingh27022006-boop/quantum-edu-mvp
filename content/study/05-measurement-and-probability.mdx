# 5. Measurement & Probability

So, your qubit is spinning in a blur of superposition. What happens when you look at it to read the answer?

Think about spinning a coin on a table. If you want to know what it is, you have to slam your hand down on it to stop it. 

The moment you slam your hand down, the coin is forced to choose: it becomes exactly Heads (0) or exactly Tails (1).

In quantum computing, this is called **Measurement**. You cannot read the "blur" of a qubit. The moment you measure it, the superposition collapses, and it spits out a normal 0 or 1. 

If the coin was a perfectly balanced spin, slamming your hand down gives a 50% chance of 0, and a 50% chance of 1. Quantum computing is fundamentally based on probability!
