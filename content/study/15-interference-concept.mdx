# 15. Interference Concept

Picture a calm pond. If you throw a pebble into it, ripples spread outward. 
If you throw *two* pebbles in at the same time, their ripples collide. 

Where a peak meets a peak, the wave gets twice as high (Constructive Interference).
Where a peak meets a trough, they cancel each other out completely, leaving perfectly flat water (Destructive Interference).

Because qubits exist as waves of probability (superposition), they can interfere with each other too!

Quantum algorithms are designed to create massive ripples of probabilities. Programmers orchestrate these ripples so that all the wrong answers destructively cancel each other out, leaving only the correct answer behind.
