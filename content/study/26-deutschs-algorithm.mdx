# 26. Deutsch's Algorithm

Imagine you have a coin, and you want to know if it's fair (Heads on one side, Tails on the other) or fake (Heads on both sides).
Classically, you must look at the coin twice: once to check the front, and once to check the back.

**Deutsch's Algorithm** is a famous quantum trick that lets a quantum computer check the coin by looking at it *only once*.

By putting a qubit into superposition (a spinning coin), passing it through a black box that represents the coin, and using quantum interference, the quantum computer evaluates both sides of the coin simultaneously. It spits out the answer in a single step!

While checking a coin isn't very useful in the real world, Deutsch's Algorithm was the very first proof that quantum computers could solve certain problems faster than classical computers.
