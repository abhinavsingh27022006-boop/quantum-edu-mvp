# 24. Superdense Coding

Teleportation uses two classical bits to send one quantum bit. 
What if we do the exact opposite?

**Superdense Coding** allows Alice to send *two* classical bits of information (like `10` or `11`) to Bob, by sending him only *one* single physical qubit!

How? Again, they must share an entangled pair first.
Alice takes her qubit, and depending on the two-bit message she wants to send, she applies either nothing, an X gate, a Z gate, or both (X and Z) to her single qubit. 

She then puts her single qubit in a box and mails it to Bob. 
Bob receives the qubit, runs it through a CNOT and an H gate with his entangled half, and measures both. Magically, the two classical bits Alice wanted to send pop out! 

By manipulating the quantum phase of an entangled pair, one physical particle can carry twice as much data as classically allowed.
