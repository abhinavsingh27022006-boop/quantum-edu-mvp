# 10. Entanglement Concept

Einstein famously hated entanglement. He called it "spooky action at a distance."

When you link two qubits together in a quantum computer, their probabilities become permanently fused. They are no longer two separate spinning coins—they are one single system.

If two qubits are entangled to perfectly correlate, measuring one qubit instantly determines the state of the other, even if you separate them by thousands of miles!

In the Quantum Lab, you can try this out yourself using a **CX Gate** (Controlled-NOT gate). But for now, just appreciate how bizarre nature truly is. You've completed the first module of the Study Lab!
